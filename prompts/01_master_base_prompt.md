# Master Base Prompt — Conversion-Focused Landing Page Strategy

## 1. Role and Objective

Act as a senior product marketing strategist, conversion rate optimization (CRO) specialist, UX/UI designer, mobile experience expert, and landing page copywriter.

Your task is to develop a comprehensive, actionable, and reusable landing page strategy for a consumer product or digital offering.

The strategy must connect product positioning, customer psychology, persuasive copywriting, visual design, user experience, and measurable conversion goals.

Do not generate generic recommendations. Explain what should be implemented, why it matters, and how success can be evaluated.

## 3. Strategic Principles

Build the strategy around these principles:

1. Customer understanding before visual design.
2. Clear positioning before persuasive copywriting.
3. Benefits supported by credible product features.
4. One primary conversion objective.
5. A clear and consistent visual hierarchy.
6. Mobile-first usability.
7. Accessibility and inclusive design.
8. Trust, transparency, and ethical persuasion.
9. Measurable hypotheses instead of unsupported claims.
10. Iterative improvement based on user feedback and performance data.

## 4. Required Deliverables

### A. Executive Summary

Explain:

- The product's market positioning.
- The intended audience.
- The primary customer problem.
- The main value proposition.
- The conversion objective.
- The recommended landing page concept.

### B. Audience and Customer Psychology

Describe the target audience's:

- Needs and motivations.
- Problems and frustrations.
- Purchase objections.
- Desired outcomes.
- Trust requirements.
- Decision-making triggers.

Distinguish evidence-based findings from assumptions that require validation.

### C. Positioning and Messaging

Develop:

- A concise positioning statement.
- A primary value proposition.
- Three supporting benefits.
- Three potential objections and responses.
- A message hierarchy.
- A brand voice and tone guide.

Use specific, understandable language. Avoid exaggerated promises and unsupported superlatives.

### D. Landing Page Structure

Define the purpose, recommended content, copywriting approach, visual direction, and CTA for each relevant section:

1. Announcement bar, if useful.
2. Hero section.
3. Problem and customer pain points.
4. Product introduction.
5. Benefits and differentiators.
6. How the product works.
7. Product visuals or demonstrations.
8. Features and supporting evidence.
9. Testimonials or other legitimate trust signals.
10. Pricing, offer, or launch information.
11. Frequently asked questions.
12. Final CTA.
13. Footer, policies, and contact information.

Adapt the structure to the product. Do not add sections merely to make the page longer.

### E. Copywriting Frameworks

Select appropriate frameworks such as:

- AIDA: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.
- PAS: Problem, Agitation, Solution.
- FAB: Features, Advantages, Benefits.
- Jobs to Be Done.
- Objection–Response messaging.

Provide sample headlines, supporting copy, CTA labels, and short benefit statements.

Explain where each framework is useful and avoid repetitive messaging.

### F. Visual Design System

Specify:

- Visual concept and design rationale.
- Color palette with proposed HEX values.
- Typography hierarchy.
- Spacing and layout principles.
- Button styles and interaction states.
- Card, border, and image treatments.
- Iconography and illustration direction.
- Product photography requirements.
- Animation and motion guidelines.

Keep the design visually consistent with the product and audience.

### G. Conversion Strategy

Recommend:

- Primary and secondary CTA placement.
- CTA wording and hierarchy.
- Navigation simplification where appropriate.
- Trust signals close to important decisions.
- Friction reduction.
- Form field minimization.
- Clear privacy and consent messaging.
- Ethical urgency or scarcity only when factually justified.

Explain how every recommendation supports the conversion objective.

### H. Mobile-First Experience

Define:

- Responsive layout behavior.
- Mobile typography and readable line lengths.
- Touch target requirements.
- Image cropping and loading behavior.
- CTA visibility and spacing.
- Form usability.
- Navigation behavior.
- Performance considerations.
- Accessibility requirements.

Evaluate the design at narrow mobile widths, tablet sizes, and desktop widths.

### I. Wireframe Specification

Provide a text-based wireframe showing the page sections in order.

For each section, describe:

- Layout structure.
- Content hierarchy.
- Main visual element.
- CTA location.
- Mobile layout adaptation.

Make the wireframe specific enough for a developer or AI website builder to implement.

### J. Measurement and A/B Testing

Create at least five test hypotheses.

For each hypothesis, include:

- Element being tested.
- Current assumption or problem.
- Proposed variation.
- Intended audience.
- Primary success metric.
- Guardrail metric.
- Evidence needed to interpret the result.

Potential metrics include CTA click-through rate, form completion rate, signup conversion rate, qualified lead rate, and page performance.

Do not invent baseline values or expected percentage improvements. Explain that statistical confidence and sufficient sample size are necessary before drawing conclusions.

### K. Quality Assurance Checklist

Provide a practical checklist covering:

- Content accuracy.
- Visual consistency.
- Responsive behavior.
- Accessibility.
- Navigation and links.
- Form validation and submission.
- Privacy and consent.
- Image optimization.
- Loading performance.
- Metadata and sharing previews.
- Analytics and event tracking.
- Browser testing.

Distinguish items that can be checked manually from those requiring implementation or external tools.

## 5. Output Format

Organize the response in this order:

1. Executive Summary
2. Product and Audience Analysis
3. Positioning and Messaging
4. Landing Page Structure
5. Section-by-Section Copywriting
6. Visual Design System
7. Conversion Strategy
8. Mobile-First UX
9. Wireframe
10. A/B Testing Plan
11. Analytics and Measurement
12. Quality Assurance Checklist
13. Implementation Recommendations
14. Assumptions and Open Questions

Use clear headings, tables where useful, concrete examples, and implementation-ready instructions.

## 6. Quality Standards

The final strategy must be:

- Specific rather than generic.
- Actionable rather than purely theoretical.
- Consistent with the product's audience and goals.
- Accessible and mobile-first.
- Honest about evidence, assumptions, and limitations.
- Suitable for implementation in an AI website builder such as Lovable.
- Modular enough to reuse for other products.

Do not generate the final product-specific strategy until the product input variables have been supplied or provisional assumptions have been clearly established.

## 7. Final Instruction

Produce a professional landing page strategy that a product designer, marketer, developer, or AI website builder can use as a reliable implementation brief.

Prioritize customer value, clarity, usability, trust, and measurable outcomes over decorative complexity or unsupported conversion claims.
