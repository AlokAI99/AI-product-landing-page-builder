# Product Discovery Prompt — Landing Page Requirements

## Role

Act as a product discovery strategist, customer research analyst, product marketing specialist, and conversion-focused UX consultant.

Your task is to transform an initial product idea into a clear, structured product brief that can be used to design a high-quality landing page.

## 1. Product Idea

Use the following information:

- Product name: [PRODUCT_NAME]
- Product category: [PRODUCT_CATEGORY]
- Initial product idea: [PRODUCT_IDEA]
- Target audience: [TARGET_AUDIENCE]
- Main customer problem: [CUSTOMER_PROBLEM]
- Proposed solution: [PROPOSED_SOLUTION]
- Differentiation: [DIFFERENTIATION]
- Budget or pricing: [BUDGET_OR_PRICING]
- Brand personality: [BRAND_PERSONALITY]
- Design preferences: [DESIGN_PREFERENCES]
- Business stage: [BUSINESS_STAGE]

If a field is unknown, do not stop the workflow. Make a clearly labeled provisional assumption and identify what needs validation.

## 2. Product Understanding

Explain:

1. What the product is.
2. Who it is designed for.
3. What problem it solves.
4. How customers currently address that problem.
5. Why a customer might choose this product.
6. What evidence is needed to establish demand.

Separate confirmed facts, assumptions, and research questions.

## 3. Target Audience

Define the primary and secondary audience segments.

For each segment, describe:

- Relevant characteristics.
- Needs and desired outcomes.
- Frustrations and pain points.
- Purchase motivations.
- Potential objections.
- Factors that build trust.
- Likely reasons for abandoning the purchase.

Do not stereotype users or present unsupported demographic assumptions as facts.

## 4. Value Proposition

Develop:

- One concise value proposition.
- Three supporting customer benefits.
- Three differentiating product attributes.
- A short positioning statement.
- A one-sentence elevator pitch.
- Three potential hero headlines.
- Three supporting subheadlines.

Explain which options are strongest and why.

Do not claim superiority, guaranteed results, or unique features without supporting evidence.

## 5. Product Features and Scope

Organize proposed features into:

- Essential launch features.
- Useful secondary features.
- Future enhancements.
- Features requiring further validation.

For each essential feature, explain its customer benefit and how it could be demonstrated on the landing page.

## 6. Business and Conversion Goals

Evaluate potential objectives, including:

- Email signups.
- Product pre-orders.
- Purchases.
- Free-trial registrations.
- Demo requests.
- Waitlist registrations.
- Design voting or community engagement.

Recommend one primary conversion goal and relevant secondary goals.

Explain the user journey from the first page visit to the intended action.

Do not assume that every conversion type is suitable for every product.

## 7. Offer and Trust

Recommend appropriate approaches to:

- Pricing communication.
- Product bundles or launch offers.
- Refund and shipping information.
- Frequently asked questions.
- Privacy and consent.
- Customer reviews and testimonials.
- Product demonstrations.
- Guarantees, only where genuinely supported.

Clearly distinguish available evidence from proposed future assets.

Never fabricate customer feedback or artificial scarcity.

## 8. Design Direction

Propose:

- A visual concept.
- A suitable color palette.
- Typography direction.
- Product photography requirements.
- Image and illustration styles.
- Page layout preferences.
- Interaction ideas.
- Mobile-specific design considerations.

Explain how the design direction reflects the product and audience.

## 9. Competitive Differentiation

Identify the types of competing products and alternative solutions that should be researched.

Provide a comparison framework covering:

- Target audience.
- Main promise.
- Features.
- Pricing model.
- Visual positioning.
- Trust signals.
- Conversion experience.

If verified competitor research is unavailable, provide a research plan instead of inventing competitor findings.

## 10. Risks and Validation

Identify key assumptions that could affect the product or landing page.

For each assumption, provide:

- Why it matters.
- How to test it.
- What evidence would support it.
- What evidence would challenge it.
- The next action based on the result.

Prioritize the assumptions with the greatest impact on product demand and conversion.

## 11. Required Output

Produce a structured product brief with:

1. Executive summary.
2. Product description.
3. Target audience segments.
4. Customer pain points.
5. Value proposition and positioning.
6. Feature prioritization.
7. Primary and secondary conversion goals.
8. Offer and trust strategy.
9. Visual design direction.
10. Competitive research framework.
11. Risks and assumptions.
12. Validation plan.
13. Final recommended landing page direction.

## Final Instruction

Answer all questions that can reasonably be addressed from the supplied information. Make clearly labeled assumptions where necessary instead of asking a long list of questions.

The final output must be practical, internally consistent, and ready to inform a product-specific landing page master prompt.

Prioritize clarity, customer value, ethical persuasion, and evidence-based decisions.
